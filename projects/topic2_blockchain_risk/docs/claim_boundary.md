# Topic 2 Claim Boundary

This module studies blockchain market-integrity risk as a public-market alert benchmark.

It does not identify individual wallets, does not attribute fraud to specific actors, and does not provide law-enforcement-grade forensic conclusions.

The main empirical design is:

public incident -> event window -> weak label -> risk-score evaluation.

Incident-window labels are weak labels. They are useful for evaluating whether risk scores become elevated around public incidents, but they are not verified fraud ground truth.

Transparent risk scores, anomaly ensembles, weak-supervision outputs, spillover diagnostics, change-point detection, and EVT/POT tail-risk tables should be interpreted as market-integrity alert evidence.

They should not be described as proof of manipulation, proof of fraud, or proof of causal on-chain transmission.

Stablecoin depeg risk, exchange failure risk, protocol exploit risk, regulatory-action risk, and broad market stress are related but distinct risk concepts. They should not be merged into a single fraud label.
